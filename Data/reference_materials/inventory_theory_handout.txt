COURSE HANDOUT: INVENTORY THEORY & ECONOMIC ORDER QUANTITY (EOQ)

1. The Classic EOQ Model
The Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) model determines the optimal order quantity that minimizes the sum of inventory holding costs and ordering costs.

Core Assumptions:
- Annual demand (D) is known, constant, and deterministic over the planning horizon.
- Ordering cost (S) per order is fixed regardless of order quantity.
- Unit purchase cost (C) is constant with no quantity discounts.
- Holding cost per unit per year (H or hC) is known and linear.
- Lead time is zero or constant.
- Entire order arrives in a single replenishment batch; stockouts are not permitted.

2. Mathematical Formulations:
- Annual Ordering Cost = (D / Q) * S
  Where (D / Q) represents the total number of replenishment orders placed annually.
- Annual Holding (Carrying) Cost = (Q / 2) * H
  Where (Q / 2) represents the average cycle inventory maintained throughout the year.
- Total Relevant Cost (TRC) = Annual Ordering Cost + Annual Holding Cost
  TRC(Q) = (D / Q) * S + (Q / 2) * H

3. Optimal Solution:
Setting the derivative d(TRC)/dQ to 0 yields:
EOQ = Q* = sqrt((2 * D * S) / H)

Critical Equilibrium Property:
At the optimal order quantity (Q = Q*):
Annual Ordering Cost EXACTLY EQUALS Annual Holding Cost:
(D / Q*) * S = (Q* / 2) * H = sqrt((D * S * H) / 2)
Therefore, Total Relevant Inventory Cost at EOQ is:
TRC* = 2 * sqrt((D * S * H) / 2) = sqrt(2 * D * S * H)

Example Application:
Given D = 10,000 units, S = ₹500, H = ₹20:
EOQ = sqrt((2 * 10,000 * 500) / 20) = sqrt(500,000) ≈ 707.11 units.
Annual Ordering Cost = (10,000 / 707.11) * 500 = ₹7,071.07.
Annual Holding Cost = (707.11 / 2) * 20 = ₹7,071.10.
Total Relevant Cost = ₹7,071.07 + ₹7,071.10 = ₹14,142.14.
Rounding approximations to whole integers or standard decimal fractions are standard academic practice.

4. Inventory Pooling & The Square Root Law
When multiple independent regional distribution points are consolidated into a single centralized warehouse, aggregate cycle stock and safety stock decrease proportionally to the square root of the number of locations:
Total Safety Stock (Centralized) = Total Safety Stock (Decentralized) / sqrt(N)
Where N is the number of regional holding locations, assuming uncorrelated demand.
